# Guide — English source

Each entry is a term (as the app shows it, in Devanagari here) and its explanation.

## Reading the times

**24:39\***

Times are local clock time for the city, including summer time where the city has it, on a 24-hour clock. A pañcāṅga day runs from sunrise to the next sunrise, so times after midnight keep counting: 24:39* is 00:39 on the next calendar date, still within this pañcāṅga day. In settings you can switch to a 12-hour clock.

**▸ 14:18**

A time after a name is the time it ends. "शुक्ल-तृतीया ▸14:18" means the tithi runs until 14:18; what follows it is shown next, often in grey.

**▸▸**

Two arrows: it is still running at the next sunrise.

**▸अहोरात्रम्**

It lasts the whole pañcāṅga day, from sunrise to the next sunrise.

**(20-16)**

The figure in brackets on the Detail page is the same moment in ghaṭikās and vighaṭikās (palas) after sunrise. By day a ghaṭikā is one-thirtieth of the time from sunrise to sunset; by night, one-thirtieth of the time from sunset to sunrise. So 20-16 is 20 ghaṭikās and 16 palas into the day; figures of 30 and above fall in the night.

**(\*55-30)**

A star before the ghaṭikā figure, and a grey name, mark an anga that ended before today's sunrise, late in the previous night. It is shown when it ended in the last three muhūrtas of that night.

**अवमा**

Marked when a tithi or nakṣatra begins and ends between two sunrises, so that it is never the one at sunrise (a "lost" tithi).

**त्रिदिनस्पृक्**

Marked when a tithi touches three days: it is running at three successive sunrises.

**12:05–13:40**

A range is a period, from the first time to the second.

## The head of the page

**संवत्सरः**

The year in the cycle of sixty, beginning with Prabhava, followed by the name of the year in the five-year cycle (saṁvatsara, parivatsara, idāvatsara, anuvatsara, udvatsara), which is set by where the Moon stands at the start of the year.

**अयनम् · ऋतुः**

Uttarāyaṇa or dakṣiṇāyana, and the season, both reckoned from the tropical (sāyana) position of the Sun.

**सौरमासः**

The solar month is the rāśi the Sun is in (sidereal), with the day of that month in large type. On the day the Sun moves into the next rāśi, the previous rāśi and the time of the change are shown under it.

**चान्द्रमासः**

The lunar month, by the new-moon-to-new-moon (amānta) reckoning, after the crescent; "adhika" marks an intercalary month. After the zodiac wheel comes the tropical month name (madhu, mādhava …).

**वासरः**

The day of the week, by its planet; it runs from sunrise to sunrise.

**☼ ☾**

Sunrise, midday and sunset; moonrise and moonset. A dash means the Moon does not rise or set before the next sunrise.

## The five angas

**तिथिः**

The lunar day: each 12° that the Moon gains on the Sun. Fifteen tithis of the bright half (śukla, ending with pūrṇimā) and fifteen of the dark half (kṛṣṇa, ending with amāvāsyā). The moon beside it shows the phase. The tithi named for a day is the one at sunrise.

**नक्षत्रम्**

The Moon's mansion: its sidereal position in one of twenty-seven parts of 13°20′.

**योगः**

The sum of the sidereal positions of the Sun and the Moon, in twenty-seven parts of 13°20′.

**करणम्**

Half a tithi. Seven karaṇas repeat through the month; four (śakuni, catuṣpāt, nāga, kiṁstughna) come once, around the new moon.

**लग्नानि**

The rāśi rising on the eastern horizon, each with the time it ends.

## Parts of the day

**ब्राह्मम्**

Brāhma muhūrta: the fourteenth of the fifteen muhūrtas of the night, the one before the last before sunrise.

**प्रातः · सङ्गवः · मध्याह्नः · अपराह्णः · सायाह्नः**

The time from sunrise to sunset in five equal parts. The Detail page gives the time each begins.

**प्रातः सन्ध्या**

The three sandhyāvandana periods: morning, from the last muhūrta of the night through the fourth of the day; midday (mādhyāhnika), the sixth to the thirteenth muhūrtas of the day; evening, the last muhūrta of the day and the first of the night. On the Detail page the time in brackets is the earlier, secondary (gauṇa) start.

**शयनम्**

The time for going to sleep: from the end of the first quarter of the night to three-eighths of the way through it.

**दिनान्तः**

Five-eighths of the way from sunset to the next sunrise.

**घटिकाः**

On today's Kālas page, a live clock gives the time since sunrise in ghaṭikās, vighaṭīs and tatparas (each sixty of the next), from the pañcāṅga's own sunrise and sunset, after the ghaṭikā clock at ghatika-clock.web.app. On the dial sunrise is at the top, the day on the right half and the night on the left.

**मुहूर्ताः**

Day and night are each divided into fifteen named muhūrtas; the Detail page gives the time each ends.

**होराः**

Day and night are each divided into twelve horās, each ruled by a graha, the first by the lord of the weekday.

## Times to avoid, and good times

**राहुकालः**

The time from sunrise to sunset in eight parts; one part, fixed for each weekday, is rāhu kāla. Yamaghaṇṭa and gulika kāla are other parts of the same eight. At night the same is done from sunset to sunrise (rātrau).

**दुर्मुहूर्तः**

One or two of the fifteen daytime muhūrtas, by weekday, considered unfit for beginning anything.

**त्याज्यम्**

Tyājya (varjya): a stretch of time set by the nakṣatra (na), and also by the weekday (vā) and tithi (ti), avoided for beginning new work.

**अमृतकालः**

Amṛta kāla: a stretch within the nakṣatra considered especially auspicious.

**अमृतादि-योगः**

Amṛta, siddha or maraṇa (म॰) yoga, from the weekday and the nakṣatra.

**शूलम्**

The direction in which travel is avoided on this weekday, until the time given, and the parihāra: what to take before setting out if one must.

**चन्द्राष्टमराशिः**

The rāśi eighth from the Moon's: a day for caution for those whose janma rāśi it is.

**मौढ्यम्**

Grahas in maudhya: too close to the Sun to be seen.

## Śrāddha

**श्राद्ध-तिथिः**

The tithi whose śrāddha falls on this day, by the solar (saura) month. When two fall on one day, "tithidvayam"; when the lunar (cāndra) reckoning differs, it is shown too. Given for places in India only, as in the PDFs.

**श्राद्ध-कालाः**

The śrāddha period, the eighth to the twelfth muhūrtas of the day; the main time to begin (mukhya, the eighth muhūrta, kutapa) and the secondary one (gauṇa, the seventh).

**ऊन-श्राद्ध-योग्य-दिनानि**

Days suitable for the ūna-māsika śrāddhas, with the nakṣatra in the afternoon. A page of its own under More, for places in India.

## On the Detail page

**ताराबलम्**

Count from a person's janma nakṣatra to the day's nakṣatra: the count falls on one of nine tārās — janma, sampat, vipat, kṣema, pratyari, sādhaka, vadha, mitra, paramamitra. The table has the nakṣatras in three columns, nine apart, so each row shares a tārā and a colour: blues are favourable, greys unfavourable. The day's nakṣatra is janma; the other two in its row are anujanma (10th) and trijanma (19th).

**ग्रह-स्थितिः**

Where each graha is at sunrise, by sidereal rāśi, with degrees within the rāśi, set out in the South Indian chart (Mīna at top left, running clockwise).

## Pages under More

**पञ्चाङ्ग-पठनम्**

The day set out for reading aloud: each anga with its śloka and devatā, and the phalaśruti. The ślokas are from Śānti Kusumākara.

**वर्जयितुम् उचित-विषयाः**

The verses on what is better avoided today (oil bath, bathing, cleaning the teeth, shaving, certain foods and so on), selected by the day's angas, as in the PDFs.

**नवनायकाः**

The nine "officers" of the year (rājā, mantrī …), the graha holding each, and the traditional verse on what it portends.

## Festivals

**पर्वाणि**

Festivals and observances from the adyatithi rule collection. The Festivals tab opens on every festival of the year, at the next one; the chips select a single group, as listed at the start of the PDFs.

**muhurtadinaani**

Days suited to an oil bath (abhyaṅga), a haircut or shave (kṣaura) and beginning study (vidyārambha), each a page of its own under More, with the window on each day. They follow the rules in the muhūrta configurations: which tithis, nakṣatras and weekdays are allowed, which solar months and festivals to avoid, and which part of the day. A star means the window was shortened by those rules. For kṣaura, choosing your janma-nakṣatra tags each nakṣatra with its tārā and greys out days whose whole window has an unfavourable one; days on which chaturthī, ṣaṣṭhī or chaturdaśī falls are marked !mātā, !bhrātā or !bhaginī, as they are avoided by one whose mother, brother or sister is living. Chips at the top hide days by weekday, by these marks, or by poor tārābala.

**अनध्यायः**

Days on which Vedic study is suspended. Off by default; switch it on under Settings → Festival sources.

**पर्व-स्रोतांसि**

The festivals come from separate collections (by deity, region, tradition, temple). By default the app shows the same collections as the PDFs, without anadhyayana; others can be switched on, and any switched off, in settings.

## How it is computed

**jyotisha**

All times are computed with the open-source jyotisha library, which uses the Swiss Ephemeris, with the Citrā ayanāṁśa (Spica at 180°). Festivals are assigned by the rules in the adyatithi repository. This is the same code that produces the printed Adyatithi pañcāṅga, and a test checks that the app shows what the PDFs print.

**नगरम्**

Each city's data is computed in advance for three years, and rebuilt every week. Other places will come later.
